Fun Fall Recipes and Activities

Crafts

Pumkin's are a staple in halloween decorating but did you know there is a wanay to make a perfect autumn pumkin for Thanksgiving? Plaid pumpkins are in style this fall. Nothing says "cozy" like plaid and what better way to decorate your home this season.

This project is customizable and is a perfect craft to do with the whole family. First you'd need to mark out where you want your pattern to go with a pencil. Then carefully and safely carve into the marked squares. Then using acrylic paint, make any plaid pattern. It's as easy as that! Now you have a perfect cozy pumpkin to sit on your front porch.

Materials Needed:

  • A pumpkin
  • Pencil
  • Acrylic paints
  • Linoleum cutter

Treats

Everybody loves a fall treat. The cinnamon and pumpkin spice flavours melt together perfectly. With this sweet treat, you get the best of both worlds. The cookie is soft and packed with fall flavours that melt in your mouth.

These pumpkin swirl cookies will make you want to read a good book by the fire. They are a perfect addition to any Fall party and a great treat to have on a chilly day.

Steps:

  1. Whisk together the flour, baking powder and salt in a medium bowl; set aside.
  2. Beat together the granulated sugar and butter in a stand mixer fitted with the paddle attachment. Add the egg and beat to fully combine. Add the vanilla and beat to combine.Add half of the dry ingredients and turn the mixer to low; beat until incorporated.
  3. Transfer the dough to a piece of plastic wrap. Use a rubber spatula to spread and press the dough into a flat rectangle. Add a second piece of plastic wrap on top, and flatten the rectangle using a rolling pin to approximately 8 by 10 inches. Wrap tightly and refrigerate at least 2 hours and up to overnight.
  4. For the pumpkin cookie dough: Whisk together the flour, pumpkin pie spice, cinnamon, baking soda and salt in a medium bowl; set aside.
  5. Beat together the brown sugar and butter in a stand mixer. Add the egg and beat to fully combine. Add the pumpkin and vanilla and beat to combine.Add half the dry ingredients and beat on low speed until incorporated.
  6. Transfer the dough to a piece of plastic wrap. Use a rubber spatula to spread and press the dough into a flat rectangle. Add a second piece of plastic wrap on top and flatten with a rolling pin to approximately 8 inches by 10 inches. Wrap tightly and refrigerate at least 2 hours and up to overnight.
  7. Preheat the oven to 350 degrees F and line 2 baking sheets with parchment.
  8. Unwrap the pumpkin cookie dough and place on a lightly floured piece of parchment. Lightly flour the top and a rolling pin and roll into a larger rectangle about 10 1/2 by 16 inches (see Cook's Note). Slide the parchment with the dough onto a baking sheet and refrigerate while you repeat the process with the sugar cookie dough. Roll and refrigerate the sugar cookie dough in the same manner.
  9. lining up the 2 rectangles as closely as possible. Cut the dough in half crosswise with a very sharp knife or pastry cutter so you now have 2 rectangles Stack the dough rectangles on top of one another so you now have 4 layers of alternating cookie dough. Cut this stack crosswise into 6 rows, then lengthwise into 4 rows so you end up with a total of 24 squares.Stack one layered square on top of a second one and, using lightly floured hands, gently press the edges together and round into a domed ball. Place on the prepared baking sheet. Repeat with the remaining squares, evenly spacing 6 dough balls on each lined baking sheet.
  10. Bake, rotating the baking sheets halfway through, until the cookies are puffed in the center and golden brown around the edges, 25 to 30 minutes. Let cool on the pans 10 minutes, then transfer to a wire rack set inside a baking sheet to cool completely.
